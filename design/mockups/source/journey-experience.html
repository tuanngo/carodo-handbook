Explore rows — four ways to make them less alike (V7)

The same two days in four row designs. What makes V5 read as a table: every row opens with the same tile, every route fills its tile whatever its length, and the row ends in a chevron, so the eye sees a grid of identical boxes. Each variant changes one or two of those.

2 + 3 combined (owner, 2026-11-14)

Time column on the left (secondary ink: the anchor, not the accent), title with its place symbol, driving time (and a milestone) under it, the route sized by distance, and the distance as the trailing figure, always there. The day header adds the day's kilometres. Rows breathe more (12 pt), since a day holds few drives. Every row reads left to right: when · where · how long · what shape · how far.

2 + 3, unboxed route (V9, owner 2026-11-14)

No frame for any journey, not even an invisible square: each route is drawn in its own proportions (a tall road trip stays tall, a wide commute wide), sized by distance, anchored just before the kilometres, free to spill above and below its row into the space between rows, and faded out toward the title with the Feed's mask, so it never sits under a word at full strength.

    Day — two cases (V4, owner 2026-11-14)

    Several drives: the compact timeline (dot and hairline rail at the edge; time range in Copper, title, distance · driving time) over one frame that draws every route of the day together, on their real geography (home, school and back meet where they really meet), behind the rows from the first to the last. The real map stays in Journey Detail.

    The Route Glyph — rules and edge cases

    Each cell is the glyph at 44 pt (rows) and 56 pt, on Light and Dark. Start: a hollow ring. End: a filled Copper dot. No place symbol in a list glyph (owner, 2026-11-14: too small); the title carries the place. A loop's ends meet: one combined mark. A very short drive is drawn at a minimum span of 300 m, so a car-park shuffle stays small in the middle. No route at all: a quiet dash, never a made-up line.

    One route family, three audiences — should Feed and share card share the concept?

    Recommendation: one visual family, three separate concepts. They may share drawing code (stroke, caps, the end-mark vocabulary, one projection and fitting step) but never their geometry rules, because each answers to a different audience and privacy rule.
